**Status:** designed, not scheduled. **Design from:** new. Only the money manager needs it.

## What it is

The money manager shows a person their accounts and spending in one place. This package connects to their bank, imports balances and transactions, and tidies them into something usable.

## Requirements

- The person connects a bank through their bank's own consent screen. We never see their bank password.
- Connections expire and must be renewed; the person is reminded before that happens.
- Transactions are imported once even if a sync runs twice, in whole minor units.
- Categories come from rules first; automatic suggestions are optional and show their reason.
- Bank tokens are encrypted and never logged.

## Design

- **A provider interface** with one adapter to start (GoCardless Bank Account Data, TrueLayer or Plaid are the candidates; which one is decided on UK bank coverage, price and the licence they operate under).
- **Sync as a job** in `@repo/jobs`: a daily pull per connection, with backoff, and a clear state when the bank asks the person to reconnect.
- **De-duplication** by the bank's own transaction id, with a fallback key for banks that do not give a stable one.
- **Amounts as `Money`**, signs normalised (money out is negative).
- **Categorisation rules** are data the person edits, applied in order; a transaction keeps which rule matched.
- **Tokens** are stored encrypted with a key outside the database.

## Trade-offs

- Provider coverage and uptime are outside our control, so the UI must show "last updated" honestly.
- Automatic categorisation by an AI model would send transaction text to a third party. If it is added, it is opt-in and uses minimal, redacted text.

## Open questions

- **Regulation.** Reading bank accounts is a regulated activity in the UK. Whether you need your own registration or can work under a provider's licence is a legal question to settle before building. I cannot answer it.
